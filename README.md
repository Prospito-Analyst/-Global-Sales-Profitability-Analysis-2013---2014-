# -Global-Sales-Profitability-Analysis-2013---2014-
An End to End analysis of a Sales data using only Excel.
# 📄 Introduction
This is a data analysis project for a global manufacturing company. They sell various products (like Paseo, VTT, and Velo) across five major countries (USA, Canada, France, Germany, and Mexico) to different business segments (Government, Small Business, Enterprise, etc.).

I was given their Sales dataset covering the years 2013 and 2014. My job was to clean the data, calculate the exact profit and loss the business is making, and build a dashboard to help management make better financial decisions.

# ❓ The Problem
The company had thousands of sales records, but they could not easily see the big picture. Management needed clear answers to major business questions like:

How much total revenue and profit did we make?

Are there any products causing us to lose money?

Which country and business segment are buying the most products?

How much money are we giving away in discounts?

They needed the raw data cleaned, analyzed, and summarized into an easy-to-read dashboard.

# 🛠️ The Analysis (Tasks Completed & Formulas Used)
To solve the problem, I completed several tasks using Excel to clean the data, separate the metrics, and find the answers.

**1. Data Cleaning**

Checked the raw dataset for missing values and ensured all columns had the correct data types (like changing date columns to short dates and sales columns to standard currency).

Created a completely separate clean_dataset sheet to keep the cleaned working data safe from the raw data.

**2. Doing the Math (Calculated Columns)**
I created new columns to figure out the exact costs, profits, and losses using these math formulas:

Gross Sales: =Units Sold * Sale Price

Net Sales: =Gross Sales - Discounts

Clean COGS (Cost of Goods Sold): =Units Sold * Manufacturing Price

Profit/Loss: =Sales - clean_cogs

Separating Profit and Loss: I created an IF formula to separate positive profits and negative losses into two different columns for easier tracking.

Profit Column: =IF([@[profit/loss]]>0, [@[profit/loss]], 0)

Loss Column: =IF([@[profit/loss]]<0, [@[profit/loss]], 0)

**3. Summarizing Data (Pivot Tables)**
I used Pivot Tables in an analysis sheet to group the massive amount of data into small, easy-to-read summaries:

Pivot Table 1: Total Sales and Profit grouped by Country.

Pivot Table 2: Sales grouped by Product and Segment.

Pivot Table 3: Total Loss generated by each specific Product.

**4. Building the Dashboard**
I created dashboard sheets with charts and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) showing:

Key Numbers: Total Sales, Total Profit, Total Loss, Total Discounts, and Total Units Sold.

Charts: Bar charts to show sales by country and segment, and charts to clearly highlight which products were losing the company money.

# 🙋‍♂️ Key Questions & Answers
Here are the main questions the managers needed answered, and how my project answered them:

**Q: Which country generates the highest sales?**

**Answer:** The USA is the highest, bringing in $25.02 Million. Canada is a very close second with $24.88 Million.

**Q: Which business segment buys the most?**

**Answer:** The Government segment brings in the most money by far ($52.5 Million), followed by Small Businesses ($42.4 Million).

**Q: Which product is our best seller?**

**Answer:** Paseo is the top-selling product, generating $33.01 Million in total sales.

**Q: Are we losing money on any products?**

**Answer:** Yes. Even though the company makes a lot of profit, they also generated a massive $58.9 Million in total losses across certain bad transactions. The product Amarilla caused the biggest loss (-$24.9 Million), followed closely by VTT (-$24.5 Million).

# 💡 Key Insights
From looking at all the numbers and charts, here is what I discovered:

**High Sales but High Losses:** The company generated a massive $118.7 Million in total sales. They made $71 Million in pure profit, but they also lost $58.9 Million in bad deals. This means almost half of the potential profit was wiped out by losses.

**Government is Key:** The Government sector alone makes up almost half of the entire company's revenue ($52.5 Million out of $118.7 Million).

**Problem Products:** The Amarilla and VTT products are heavily driving the company's financial losses.

**Discounts are Costly:** The company gave away $9.2 Million in total discounts, which might be a major reason why some products are selling at a loss.

# 🚀 Recommendations
Based on the data, here is what the company should do next:

**Investigate Amarilla and VTT Products:** Management must urgently check why Amarilla and VTT are losing so much money. The manufacturing cost is too high compared to some of the Sales Price it was sold at. They are also being discounted too heavily. They need to fix the pricing strategy or maintain a consistent pricing or stop producing them entirely.

**Review the Discount Strategy:** Giving away $9.2 Million in discounts is severely eating into the profit margins. The sales team should be trained to rely less on discounts, especially for products that already cost a lot to manufacture.

**Focus on the Government Segment:** Since the Government segment buys the most, the marketing and sales teams should focus heavily on winning more government contracts, as this is currently their safest and a profitable sector. Maintaining a good Profit-Pricing for the Government sector would also cut its losses too as its a major Segments where losses are.

**Boost the Paseo Product:** Paseo is the clear winner in sales and does not cause major losses. The company should increase the production and marketing efforts for Paseo.
